From genome to medicine

Every new drug begins as a question about the human body. Genomics — the science of reading our DNA — has become the sharpest tool we have for asking it. Here is how a strand of code becomes a treatment, step by step.

The one idea to hold onto

Your genome is an instruction manual. Disease is often a typo.

Genomics-driven drug discovery is really a search for typos that matter — and then a plan to work around them. Three plain-language pieces make the whole thing click.

01 — the manual

The genome

Roughly 3 billion letters (A, T, C and G) that spell out how to build and run a human body. Nearly every cell carries a full copy.

02 — the typo

A variant

A place where your spelling differs from the reference. Most are harmless. A few change how a protein works — and that can cause, or protect against, disease.

03 — the fix

A drug

A molecule designed to turn a protein up, down, or off — correcting for the typo's effect. Genetics tells us which protein to aim at.

Why bother with genetics?

Genetic evidence roughly doubles the odds

Most drug candidates fail. But when the target was chosen because human genetics pointed to it, the candidate is far more likely to survive trials and reach patients. Flip the switch.

Baseline chance of reaching approval ~1×

Baseline. A target with no genetic support is essentially a well-reasoned bet. Historically only a small fraction of such programmes make it all the way through.

Beyond finding the target

Genomics keeps working after the drug exists

The same DNA data that reveals a target also helps match the right drug to the right person — the heart of precision medicine.

Precision medicine

The right patients

Genomic markers can predict who will respond. Trials enrol the patients most likely to benefit, so effects are clearer and smaller trials can succeed.

Companion diagnostics

A test with the pill

Many modern drugs ship with a genetic test that confirms a patient carries the variant the drug was designed for — approved together by regulators.

Pharmacogenomics

The right dose

Your genes affect how you break down medicines. Genomics flags who needs a lower dose or a different drug to stay safe.

New modalities

Editing the source

Reading DNA also lets us write it. Gene therapies and CRISPR-based treatments aim to fix the typo directly, rather than working around it.
